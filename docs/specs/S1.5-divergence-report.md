## 3. The non-divergence: gross R ≈ legacy R

The first thing to explain is what *didn't* diverge. On 7 of 11 trades, gross
option R matches legacy underlying R to two decimals.

**This is structural, not coincidental.** For a small underlying move, option
P&L ≈ Δ × move, and the modelled premium risk ≈ Δ × stop distance. Delta appears
in both numerator and denominator and cancels:

```
gross R  =  (Δ × move) / (Δ × stop_distance)  =  move / stop_distance  =  legacy R
```

**Conclusion: the underlying-denominated proxy is not, by itself, the error.**
Over holds this short it reproduces option-denominated R to within a few
percent. The problem was never the denomination — it is that the number was
**gross**. That is a meaningful correction to how review item 2 was framed.

Where the two do separate, it is **gamma**, and the direction is the one S1.1 §5
predicted from convexity: deviations favour the holder. The two largest moves
show it — AMZN +0.74 → +0.81 and SPCX +1.13 → +1.20 — and the stop-out shows the
mirror image, −1.88 → −1.85, a loss slightly *smaller* than linear. Convexity is
real and measurable here, but at ~4–6% of R it is an order of magnitude below
friction.

---

## 4. Why net R diverges: friction enters twice

`r_multiple_net` differs from gross for two independent reasons, and they push
in opposite directions:

1. **Numerator** — round-trip friction is subtracted from P&L. Always adverse.
2. **Denominator** — 1R is redefined as the *all-in* cost of a stop-out
   (premium loss + both legs' friction), which is larger than the mid-to-mid
   premium risk. This *shrinks* the magnitude of every R, win or loss.

For winners both effects compound: AMZN +0.74 → +0.27, SPCX +1.13 → +0.36.

For losers they fight. That is why **two of the eleven gaps are negative** —
legacy was *more* pessimistic than net. This is the single most
counterintuitive result in the report and it is explained individually below.

> ⚠️ **`r_multiple_net` and `r_multiple` are not comparable quantities and must
> never share an axis.** They use different units of risk. The aggregate
> comparison in §1 is legitimate because both are per-trade means over the same
> trades; a per-trade scatter would be misleading.

## 5. Every material gap, explained

**The two sign flips — NFLX +0.04 → −0.22 and +0.10 → −0.25.**
These were never wins. Gross option P&L was **+$1.36** and **+$2.89**, against
friction of $12.76 and $14.30. The underlying moved 3¢ and 6¢ over 6–7 minutes —
below the bid/ask spread of the contract being traded. Legacy R recorded them as
wins because 3¢ ÷ a 74¢ stop distance is a positive number. **The R was real;
the profit never existed.** These two rows are the clearest demonstration of why
gross R is not a performance metric.

**SPCX CALL stop-out, −1.88 → −1.29 (gap −0.59, the largest negative).**
Entry 125.96, stop 125.38, close 124.87 — it closed **$0.51 through the stop**.
Legacy divides that overshoot by the underlying stop distance ($0.58) and gets
−1.88. Net divides the actual dollar loss by the all-in stop cost ($51.41) and
gets −1.29. The loss did not shrink — **the R unit grew.** Net R is less negative
here purely because the denominator now includes friction the legacy denominator
ignored. Friction on this trade was $28.83, or **1.42R** — the only trade where
friction exceeded one full unit of risk, driven by the 1.5× stop-exit widening.

**AAPL, −0.87 → −0.83 (gap −0.04).** The only other negative gap, and it is
small. A 1.48-point adverse move on a PUT is large enough for convexity to
cushion the premium loss meaningfully; that cushion (+$6.8 vs the linear map)
nearly cancels the $26.77 of friction in R terms. A coincidence of magnitudes,
not a pattern.

**SPY, +0.09 — the smallest positive gap.** Friction of $6.47 is by far the
lowest in the set, because the quoted spread was **0.6%** (4.85/4.88) — the
tightest contract traded. This is the control case: when the spread is genuinely
tight, the two measures nearly agree. It is also the only trade whose friction
falls below 0.2R.

**The remaining six gaps (+0.09 to +0.35)** are unremarkable and need no
individual explanation: friction of 0.4–0.8R applied to trades whose gross
outcome was modest, producing a uniform downward shift.

## 6. Sensitivity: the conclusion does not depend on the open assumptions

`entry_fill_aggression` / `exit_fill_aggression` and `stop_exit_spread_multiplier`
are §11 items 2 and 3 — still unanswered by you. They are the model's largest
free parameters, so the result was re-run across their plausible range:

| Aggression | Stop mult. | Net mean R | Winners | Friction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.00 (mid fills) | 1.00 | **−0.304** | 2/11 | $51.20 |
| 0.50 | 1.25 | **−0.386** | 2/11 | $141.20 |
| 1.00 (full cross) | 1.50 | **−0.437** | 2/11 | $221.20 |
| 1.00 | 1.00 | **−0.466** | 2/11 | $216.20 |
| *legacy, for reference* | — | *−0.260* | *4/11* | *$0* |

**Every configuration is worse than legacy, and the win count is 2/11 in all of
them.** Even assuming free mid fills — an assumption no one would defend — net R
is worse and two of the four recorded wins disappear. The two sign flips are
driven by friction that exists at *any* aggression, because gross P&L on those
trades was $1.36 and $2.89.

So §11 items 2 and 3 change the *magnitude* (−0.30 to −0.47) but not the
direction, the win count, or any conclusion in this report.

**One counterintuitive property worth recording:** raising the stop multiplier
from 1.0 → 1.5 makes net mean R *less* negative (−0.466 → −0.437). A more
pessimistic stop assumption enlarges the risk denominator faster than it
enlarges the loss. **The stop multiplier is therefore not a conservatism dial**
and must not be treated as one — raising it does not make results more
conservative.

## 9. Findings not anticipated by the spec

**1. Median hold time is 8 minutes.** Nine of eleven exits fire on
`live exit signal`. Range 6–54 minutes. This is the root cause of friction
dominance and it was not on anyone's list: 1R in premium terms is tiny because
the *hold* is tiny, so a fixed round-trip spread consumes half of it. Widening
stops or tightening spreads would both help, but the structural issue is that
the exit engine is closing positions before the geometry has room to resolve.
This is the highest-value thread in the report and it belongs to Phase 7
(exit simplification), not Phase 1.

**2. Gross performance is near scratch; friction is the whole loss.**
−$54.29 gross vs −$275.49 net. Any effort spent improving entry selection is
being spent on the smaller term.

**3. The AMZN trade opened at 8 DTE**, below the documented `OPTION_DTE_MIN` of
10 (`O:AMZN260717P00240000`, opened 2026-07-09). Either the gate did not apply or
the documented minimum is wrong. Worth one grep; not pursued here as it is out
of S1.5 scope.

**4. `option_close_mid` is NULL for all 12 archived trades** — independent
confirmation at the archive level of the §3.1(b) defect that S1.4 fixed. No exit
option quote has ever been captured, which is why every trade here had to be
BS-estimated.

## 10. Limitations — read before citing any number above

- **n = 11.** A single 8-trading-day window (2026-07-09 → 07-20), 6 symbols, one
  volatility regime. This is an *explanation* of a mechanism, not an estimate of
  expectancy. Nothing here meets any promotion or evidence gate in METRICS.md §5.
- **Every exit premium is modelled, none observed.** No `ACTUAL_QUOTE` path was
  exercised, because the archive has no exit quotes. The frozen-IV bias applies
  in full and is untestable here: S1.1 §4.2 predicts it overstates call winners
  and understates put winners. **This report cannot confirm or refute that**, and
  8 of 11 trades are directional in ways that bias would touch.
- **Tick rounding makes the aggression-0.0 row slightly pessimistic.** Fills
  round away from the trader per spec §7.2, so even a "mid fill" is rounded to
  the adverse tick. The $51.20 floor therefore overstates true zero-crossing
  friction by roughly $35. It does not affect the ordering in §6.
- **Commission is the unconfirmed $0.65 placeholder** (§11 item 1). At ~$15.50
  of $221.20 total friction it is ~7%; correcting it cannot change any
  conclusion.
- **SPCX is the unresolved ticker** flagged in Phase 4 / S4.1 and accounts for 3
  of 11 trades.
- `stop_exit_spread_multiplier = 1.5` remains an assumption, not a measurement,
  and cannot be measured until S1.4's exit-quote capture accumulates data.
