## 1. Why this phase exists

The system trades **options**. Every number it reports is denominated in the
**underlying stock**, computed with **zero transaction costs**.

`_paper_trade_result` ([METRICS.md §2](../METRICS.md#2-trade-outcome-the-paper-pl-formula)):

```
pnl  = close − entry          # underlying dollars per share
risk = entry − stop           # underlying dollars per share
r_multiple = pnl / risk
```

Nothing in that formula touches the contract that was actually bought. The
reported R is the R of a **stock trade the system never took**.

### The size of the omission

Take a representative trade at the effective $600 contract cap
([STRATEGY.md §5](../STRATEGY.md#5-option-selection)), 1 contract
(`MAX_CONTRACTS_PER_TRADE=1`), premium $6.00/share:

| Quantity | Value | As a fraction of 1R |
|---|---|---|
| Assumed 1R risk (`OPTION_STOP_LOSS_PCT` = 20% of premium) | $120 | 1.00R |
| Round-trip spread crossing at 5% spread | $30 | **0.25R** |
| Round-trip spread crossing at 8% (`TELEGRAM_MAX_SPREAD_PCT`) | $48 | **0.40R** |
| Round-trip spread crossing at 10% (`OPTION_MAX_SPREAD_PCT`) | $60 | **0.50R** |
| Round-trip commission + regulatory fees (§6 defaults) | ~$1.40 | ~0.012R |

**Friction is 0.25R–0.50R per round trip, and the spread outweighs commission by
roughly 40×.** Any cost model that gets commission exactly right and ignores the
spread has solved the wrong problem.

Propagating that to breakeven win rate, at the planned RR of 1.8:

| Scenario | Winner | Loser | Breakeven win rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gross (what is reported today) | +1.80R | −1.00R | **35.7%** |
| Net at 0.25R friction | +1.55R | −1.25R | **44.6%** |
| Net at 0.50R friction | +1.30R | −1.50R | **53.6%** |

A strategy that looks comfortably profitable at a 40% win rate is underwater at
the same win rate once friction is counted. This is the failure mode
`EXECUTION_PLAN.md` describes as "plausible expectancy numbers and no error
message."

⚠️ **These figures are an order-of-magnitude motivator, not a result.** They
inherit the current 20%-of-premium stop assumption, which §5 argues is itself
wrong. They are superseded by S1.2's hand-computation and S1.5's reconciliation.

> ### ⛔ Superseded by measurement — see [§10.1a](#101a-what-the-fixtures-revealed--1s-mechanism-was-wrong)
>
> S1.2 measured this against real quotes. **The conclusion holds; the mechanism
> above is wrong.** Real liquid spreads are 1.8–3.8%, not 5–10%, and friction is
> only 2.3–5.4% of premium — but it is still **0.47R–0.87R**, because 1R is far
> smaller than this section assumes.
>
> Two errors above cancelled out: the spread was overstated ~2×, and 1R was
> overstated ~4× (the real premium loss at the stop is ~5.4% of premium, not the
> 20% `OPTION_STOP_LOSS_PCT` asserts). Do not reuse the "% of premium" framing —
> it understates friction by roughly 20×.

### 3.1 Two consequences

**(a) Realized option P&L cannot be computed from stored data today.** Finding 2
means there is no exit premium to subtract the entry premium from. This is the
single biggest gap, and it means BS repricing is required even for trades that
already closed — not merely for counterfactuals.

**(b) A suspected stale-field defect, to be confirmed in S1.3.** At
`paper_trade_manager.py:833-834` the exit alert is called with:

```
current_price=close_price,                      # underlying at exit — correct
option_current_mid=trade.get("option_mid"),     # ← set at OPEN, at line 670
```

Within this file `option_mid` is assigned only inside `open_paper_trade` and is
never reassigned before that read. If no other module refreshes it in place, the
"current" option mid at exit **is the entry mid**, and any option-denominated
number derived from it is structurally ~0%.

This is recorded as a **verification item, not a confirmed bug** — a dashboard
quote-refresh path could be mutating the trade dict. S1.3 must settle it. If
confirmed, the fix belongs to S1.4 (it is a measurement defect, not V1 decision
logic, so I1 permits it), and it must be fixed *before* S1.5 reconciliation,
because it would otherwise appear as a spurious divergence.

## 4. Design decision: Black-Scholes repricing

`EXECUTION_PLAN.md` §Phase 1 requires this choice be made here.

**Decision: Black-Scholes repricing from entry-implied volatility. Adopted.**
Historical option-quote replay is deferred to Phase 5.

| | BS repricing | Historical quote replay |
|---|---|---|
| Effort | ~1 day | Weeks (vendor, storage, backfill) |
| Accuracy over an intraday hold | ±10–20% of the premium change | High |
| Works on already-closed trades | **Yes** — needs only stored entry data | Only if quotes were archived (they were not) |
| Blocks Phase 1 | No | Yes |

Rationale: the current linear map is not merely imprecise, it has the **wrong
shape** (§5). BS with frozen IV is wrong in magnitude but right in shape. Moving
from wrong-shape to right-shape is the large gain; the residual magnitude error
is the small one, and Phase 5 collects the data to close it.

### 4.1 Getting the inputs without new storage

BS needs `S, K, T, σ, r, type`. All are obtainable from what is already stored:

| Input | Source |
|---|---|
| `S` spot | `entry_price` / `close_price` on the trade (underlying) — already stored |
| `K` strike, expiry, `type` | **Parsed from `option_ticker`.** OCC format `O:NVDA260821C00150000` encodes root, YYMMDD expiry, C/P, and strike×1000 |
| `T` | (expiry − as_of) in years, using a calendar-day convention (§7.3) |
| `σ` | **Inverted from the stored entry mid** — see below |
| `r` | Config constant, default 0.04, annualised |

**Invert IV from the entry premium rather than reading a stored IV field.** The
entry mid, spot, strike, expiry and type are all known at entry, so σ is
recoverable by numerically inverting BS. This is strictly better than depending
on `Option IV`:

1. It has no dependency on a field that finding 4 suggests is not on the trade record.
2. It is **self-consistent by construction** — the model reproduces the entry
   premium exactly, so entry P&L is identically zero and all error is confined
   to the *change*, which is what is being measured.
3. It absorbs any provider IV convention mismatch.

Where the stored `Option IV` *is* present, compute both and record the gap as a
diagnostic — a large divergence indicates a stale or mismatched quote.

### 4.2 Known biases — state them, do not hide them

| Bias | Direction | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Frozen σ** | Overstates long-call winners, understates long-put winners | Equity IV rises as spot falls. A winning call faces a vega headwind that frozen-σ BS ignores; a winning put gets a tailwind it also ignores |
| **Frozen σ on losers** | Understates the cushion | On an adverse move for a call, rising IV partly offsets delta loss |
| **Calendar-day `T`** | Slightly overstates decay over weekends | Vol time ≠ calendar time; a trading-day convention is a candidate refinement |
| **No early exercise** | Negligible | European BS on American options; long-only, no dividends captured, held intraday |

The first row is a *directional* bias, not noise — it will show up in S1.5 as a
systematic call/put asymmetry. Predicting it here is what makes S1.5 a test
rather than a rationalisation.

## 5. Definitions: what 1R means for an option

The current model asserts a stop-out costs exactly `OPTION_STOP_LOSS_PCT` = 20%
of premium, regardless of delta, DTE, or IV
(`position_sizing.py`, `option_affordability.py`). That is a constant standing in
for a function.

**It is wrong in shape, not just in value.** A long option has positive
convexity for the holder:

- On an **adverse** move, delta shrinks as it goes against you, so the premium
  loss is **less** than linear.
- On a **favourable** move, delta grows, so the premium gain is **more** than
  linear.

The real payoff is convex; the model is a straight line through it. The
consequence is that the linear map **overstates stop-out losses and understates
target gains** — before friction and theta, which push the other way. Two
material errors in opposite directions is exactly the configuration that
produces a plausible-looking net number for the wrong reasons.

### 5.1 The definitions this spec adopts

```
premium_at_stop   = BS(S = stop_price,   σ = entry_iv, T = T_entry − hold)
premium_at_target = BS(S = target_price, σ = entry_iv, T = T_entry − hold)

risk_dollars_net  = (entry_fill − stop_fill) × 100 × contracts
                    + entry_costs + exit_costs          # what a stop actually costs

r_multiple_gross  = (exit_mid    − entry_mid ) × 100 × contracts / risk_dollars_gross
r_multiple_net    = (exit_fill   − entry_fill) × 100 × contracts − costs
                    ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
                                    risk_dollars_net
```

**1R = what a stop-out actually costs, all-in.** That keeps R's intent ("multiples
of what I risked") while denominating it in the traded instrument. Both the
numerator and the denominator move relative to today's numbers, so
`r_multiple_net` is **not** comparable to `r_multiple` and must never be plotted
on the same axis without a label.

Expected consequences, to be checked in S1.5 rather than assumed:

- Net R is worse than gross R on essentially every trade.
- The net-R distribution is **more negatively skewed** than the underlying-R
  distribution: exit spreads widen precisely when stops trigger (§6.3).
- Realized RR is materially below the 1.8–2.0 planned at scan time.

### 6.3 The fill model

The most consequential modelling choice in this spec, since §1 shows the spread
dominates.

```
half_spread = (ask − bid) / 2 × spread_multiplier
fill_buy    = mid + aggression × half_spread
fill_sell   = mid − aggression × half_spread
```

then rounded **away from the trader** to `tick_size` (§7.2).

**Default `aggression = 1.0` (full cross) on both legs.** Rationale: the system
has no order-routing logic, no limit-order patience model, and no fill-rate
evidence. Assuming mid fills would embed an unearned edge of exactly the
magnitude §1 identifies as decisive. A pessimistic default is falsifiable by
evidence later; an optimistic default silently manufactures the result Phase 1
exists to test. Setting `aggression = 0.0` reproduces today's implicit
assumption and must be supported for reconciliation.

**`stop_exit_spread_multiplier = 1.5`** because a stop-out sells into an adverse,
faster-moving market where market-maker spreads widen. Applied **only** when
`exit_reason` indicates a stop. Also a placeholder — a genuine measurement of it
requires the exit-quote capture that finding 2 says does not exist yet, so it
should be treated as a **sensitivity parameter** in S1.5, not a known constant.

### 7.2 Rounding

| Quantity | Rule |
|---|---|
| Intermediate arithmetic | **Full float precision. No rounding.** |
| Fill prices | Round to `tick_size`, **away from the trader** (buys up, sells down) |
| Money | Round to cents **once, at the boundary**, never mid-computation |
| R multiples | **Never rounded** in the returned value |
| Display | Caller's responsibility |

The existing `_paper_trade_result` does `round(pnl / risk, 2)` at computation
time, folding presentation into the number. That is a real source of spurious
divergence in S1.5 reconciliation and must not be replicated. Existing behaviour
stays untouched (I2); the new path simply does not inherit the flaw.

Tick size is $0.01 below $3.00 and $0.05 above for many classes. Model it as a
function of price, not a constant, and default to the **conservative** ($0.05)
tier when the class convention is unknown.

### 7.3 Time convention

`T = (expiry_date_at_1600ET − as_of) / 365.0`, calendar days, floored at a
small positive epsilon (never zero — BS is undefined at `T=0`; below the floor,
return intrinsic value with `status="DEGRADED"`).

Note for the Phase 1 DTE × delta study: at 14–30 DTE, a 2-hour hold consumes
~0.4% of remaining life, so theta over an intraday hold is ~0.2% of extrinsic —
**negligible, and a genuine quantitative argument in favour of the current 14–30
DTE preference** for an EOD-closed strategy. At 2–6 DTE the same hold consumes
2–6% of life. This is the seed of review item 14.

### 7.4 Error behaviour — the safety contract

Every function returns a dict carrying `status` and `reason`. Never `None`,
never a bare float, never an exception into a caller.

| `status` | Meaning | Numeric fields |
|---|---|---|
| `OK` | Fully computed | Populated |
| `DEGRADED` | Computed under a stated fallback | Populated, `reason` names the fallback |
| `UNAVAILABLE` | Cannot be computed | **`None` — never `0.0`** |

**A missing input must never produce a zero.** A zero cost, a zero P&L and a
zero R are all *plausible values* that will propagate silently into an aggregate
and bias it toward "free." Any absent quantity is `None` with a reason, and every
downstream aggregate must count and report its `UNAVAILABLE` rows rather than
skipping them. This is the specific mechanism by which a cost model produces
confident wrong answers, and it is the one rule in this spec that must not be
relaxed for convenience.

Mandatory `UNAVAILABLE` cases: missing/unparseable `option_ticker`; missing or
non-positive entry bid/ask; crossed market (`bid > ask`); expiry at or before
`as_of`; IV inversion non-convergence; non-positive spot or strike;
`contracts <= 0`.

Mandatory `DEGRADED` cases: `T` below the epsilon floor (returns intrinsic);
`tick_size` inferred rather than known; entry IV taken from stored `Option IV`
because inversion failed; `stop_exit_spread_multiplier` applied to an exit whose
reason could not be classified.

Additionally, per architectural invariant 2, no function here may raise into a
decision path. In Phase 1 nothing calls them from one; S1.4 must preserve that.

## 10.1a What the fixtures revealed — §1's mechanism was wrong

The conclusion in §1 survives. **The mechanism does not.**

§1 assumed friction bites because spreads are wide (5–10%) on expensive
contracts. Real liquid NVDA contracts quote **1.8–3.8%**, and friction is only
**2.3–5.4% of premium** — which sounds negligible.

**It is not negligible, because premium is the wrong denominator.** What matters
is friction relative to **1R**, and 1R is small: a 1.3 × ATR stop is 0.278% of
spot, which on a $5.00 option is only ~$0.27 of premium movement. The round-trip
spread is ~$0.13 of that.

| Fixture | Friction | as % of premium | **as a fraction of 1R** |
|---|---|---|---|
| `FX_WINNER` | $12.70 | 2.52% | **0.47R** |
| `FX_LOSER` | $12.98 | 2.34% | **0.49R** |
| `FX_STOPOUT` | $21.57 | 5.39% | **0.87R** |

§1's headline range of 0.25R–0.50R was right by luck. **Tight stops, not wide
spreads, are what make friction decisive.** Anything reported as "% of premium"
will understate this by roughly 20×; §9's `cost_spread_component` must therefore
be reported against R, and §6.4's `friction_pct_of_premium` is actively
misleading on its own.

### The bias is asymmetric, and it flatters the strategy

| Fixture | Reported today | Net (real) | Error |
|---|---|---|---|
| `FX_WINNER` | +2.31R | +1.17R | **overstates gain by 1.13R** |
| `FX_LOSER` | −0.46R | −0.64R | **understates loss by 0.18R** |
| `FX_STOPOUT` | −1.00R | −1.00R | **0.00R — exact** |

The two definitions **agree at exactly one point: the stop.** Both are anchored
there by construction, so error is zero on a stop-out and grows with distance
from it. Consequence: the reporting error is **invisible on the losers and
maximal on the winners** — it inflates precisely the trades contributing
positive R, and understates the ones contributing negative R. Both errors point
the same way.

### `OPTION_STOP_LOSS_PCT = 0.20` is wrong by ~4× — record only, do not act

The spec's `implied_stop_loss_pct` field (§7.1) exists to answer with a function
what the codebase answers with a constant. First measurement:

| Fixture | Premium at entry → at stop | **Implied stop-loss %** | Assumed |
|---|---|---|---|
| `FX_WINNER` | 5.0000 → 4.7297 | **5.41%** | 20% |
| `FX_LOSER` | 5.5000 → 5.2334 | **4.85%** | 20% |
| `FX_STOPOUT` | 3.9250 → 3.6766 | **6.33%** | 20% |

A 1.3 × ATR stop moves the premium ~5%, not 20%. The constant overstates the
stop-out loss by **3–4×**.

This propagates into two places, **both of which are V1 decision logic and
therefore out of bounds under I1**:

- `position_sizing.py`: `contracts = floor(max_risk / (price × 0.20 × 100))` —
  sizing is ~4× more conservative than intended.
- The affordability cap: `1000 × 0.12 / 0.20 = $600`. At a measured 5.4% the
  same risk appetite implies ~$2,200.

**Recorded, not actioned.** Three fixtures on one symbol in one IV regime is not
a basis for touching sizing, and I1 forbids it regardless until Phase 7. What
this justifies is running `implied_stop_loss_pct` across the archive in S1.5 —
if the 3–4× gap holds at scale, it is a Phase 7 deletion candidate for the
constant, and it belongs in `STRATEGY.md`'s parameter table as
`PROVENANCE: none` pending that evidence.

Breakeven win rate, winners-at-target vs losers-at-stop:

| | Winner | Loser | Breakeven win rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| As reported today | +2.31R | −1.00R | **30.2%** |
| Net of costs | +1.17R | −1.00R | **46.1%** |

A strategy showing a 40% win rate reads as **+0.32R/trade** today and is
**−0.13R/trade** in reality. That sign flip is the entire justification for
Phase 1.

### Item 2 — aggression is unmeasurable here, so report a band

There is **no broker integration and there are no real fills**, so realized
slippage-versus-mid can never be observed in this system. Aggression is not a
constant awaiting calibration — it is a permanent modelling assumption. Capturing
real exit quotes (S1.4) does not fix this: the fill model still applies an
assumed aggression *to* those quotes.

Therefore: default **1.00** (the pessimistic bound — the system has no
order-routing logic, and an optimistic default would manufacture edge), and
**every headline net-R figure must be published with the 0.00 bound alongside
it.** S1.5 §6 does this. A single net-R number without its band is not a
reportable result.

### Item 3 — the stop multiplier is disabled, not tuned

Changed from the proposed 1.5 to **1.0**, for three reasons:

1. **It is invented.** No measurement supports 1.5.
2. **It moves the headline metric counterintuitively.** S1.5 §6: raising it
   1.0 → 1.5 makes net mean R *less* negative (−0.466 → −0.437), because it
   enlarges the risk denominator faster than the loss. Anyone treating it as a
   conservatism dial gets the opposite of what they intend.
3. **It is now measurable.** S1.4 captures the exit quote on stop exits;
   comparing that spread to the entry spread yields observed widening directly.

The code path is retained. Enable it as an explicit, labelled sensitivity once
there is data — not before. This also removes one free parameter, consistent
with the Phase 7 objective.

### Item 4 — definition confirmed, but dollars become the primary unit

`1R = the all-in cost of a stop-out` is confirmed. It preserves R's meaning
while denominating it correctly.

S1.5 exposed its one weakness: because friction sits in the *denominator*, a
loser can read **less** negative under net R than under legacy R (the SPCX
stop-out: −1.88 → −1.29). The loss did not shrink; the unit grew. Two guards
are therefore mandatory and not optional:

1. **`r_multiple_net` and `r_multiple` never share an axis or a table column.**
   They are different units of risk.
2. **Dollars are the primary reporting unit for the cost model; R is secondary
   and always labelled.** S1.5's headline is stated in dollars precisely because
   dollars carry no definitional ambiguity.
