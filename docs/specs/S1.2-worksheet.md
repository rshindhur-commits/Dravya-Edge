## Step 2 — Per-trade worksheet

Copy this block three times, once per fixture.

> ### ⚠️ Sign convention — the most likely place to make an error
>
> **Option premium is always long.** You buy a contract and sell it later. A
> winning PUT has a *rising* premium, exactly like a winning CALL. The direction
> flip (`is_short`) applies **only** to the underlying lines (F6, F7) — never to
> the option P&L lines (B–E). If you flip the sign on an option premium, you have
> made the error this fixture exists to catch.

## Step 3 — Where to get the premium at stop (D1)

You cannot hand-compute Black-Scholes on a calculator — it needs the normal CDF.
Use an **independent** implementation:

- Your broker's option analysis / risk-slide tool (best — same data source)
- The CBOE options calculator
- Any spreadsheet BS implementation you trust

**Record which one you used.** This matters: S1.3 builds its own BS, and your
independent calculator is the cross-check on it. If they disagree by more than a
cent or two, one of them is wrong and that is worth finding out before the model
is wired into anything.

Inputs to give it: spot = **A2** (the stop price, not the entry), strike = A6,
expiry = A7, IV = the IV implied by your entry premium at A1 (most calculators
will solve for it — enter the entry mid B1 at spot A1 first, read off IV, then
change spot to A2), rate = 0.04, time = A8 minus the hold in A14.

If your trade has **real observed exit quotes** (A11/A12), you do not need BS for
the C block at all — only for D1.

## Step 4 — Sanity checks

Run these before pasting anything into the spec. They catch arithmetic errors
without telling you the answers.

| # | Check | If it fails |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `E5 < E3` on every trade | Friction is never negative — you dropped a cost |
| 2 | `E6 > 0` always | Same |
| 3 | Friction (E7) lands roughly 3–12% of premium | Outside that, check the spread and the ×100 multiplier |
| 4 | **`FX_STOPOUT`: if you set C = D (exit at the modelled stop), E5 = −1.00 exactly** | See below — this is the strongest check on the sheet |
| 5 | `FX_STOPOUT` with a *real* exit worse than −1.00 | Correct. The gap below −1.00 **is** your slippage measurement |
| 6 | `FX_WINNER`: E3 well below the planned RR of 1.8–2.0 | Expected — convexity and friction both bite |
| 7 | PUT fixture: E1/E4 signs match the CALL fixture's logic | If your winning PUT has negative option P&L, you flipped a sign |
| 8 | Every `× 100` present | Premium is quoted per share; contracts are 100 shares |

**On check 4** — it is an identity, not a coincidence. Net R is *defined* with the
all-in stop cost as its denominator, so a trade that exits exactly at the modelled
stop must return exactly −1.00. If it does not, there is an arithmetic error in
the B, C, or D block. This is also a unit-test assertion in S1.3.
